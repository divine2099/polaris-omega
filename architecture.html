Architecture

One system, two engines, six layers.

Designed and built by Jamike Divine, working with an AI system: one Rust workspace of about thirty components, and one small contract on chain.

Built from the bottom up. A layer does not start until the one beneath it passes its tests.

Every part below is marked with what it actually is today: live, built but switched off, or designed.

Live Runs in the program and has run on the test network Built Written and tested, not switched on Designed Planned, not built
#01

The path of one trade

Top to bottom
InputA new block on any chain

On Ethereum, Base and Optimism the system runs as an extension inside the node software; on Arbitrum and BNB Chain it runs beside the node on the same machine and reads its live feed. Either way it sees new state as the node processes it.

↓
ReadPrices straight from the exchanges' contracts

No price feed in the way. Each chain keeps a running price estimate for every asset it watches.

↓
Engine 1A gap inside one chain

Two or more pools on the same chain disagree. One transaction can take it.

Engine 2A gap between chains

The same asset, proven to be the same asset, is priced differently on two chains.

↓
CheckSimulated exactly against the real state

The trade runs in a copy of the chain's own virtual machine. A cross-chain trade is simulated as each of its transactions.

↓
GateRisk limits, staleness and the right signer

A halt switch, a gas ceiling, a capital limit and the risk of one leg settling alone. A single-transaction trade signs with one key; anything that settles in parts needs a second, independent signer.

↓
ExecuteOne stateless contract

Borrows, swaps and repays inside one transaction. If it does not end with more than it started with, it undoes itself.

ExecuteA vault with a scoped role

Inventory sits in a Safe on each chain. The trading key may call a narrow set of functions and nothing else.

↓
RecordReceipt read back, result written down

Every attempt gets one record at the moment of sending and another when it resolves, so failures are counted too.

#02

Two engines

The edge is in the second
Engine 1 · Single chain

Flash-funded, profit or nothing

Takes gaps between pools on one chain, plus liquidations on lending markets, inside one transaction funded by a loan repaid in that same transaction. It needs no capital of its own, and on one chain it is a race to be first into the block. It is the entry point and the steady base.

Trading on testnet
Engine 2 · Between chains

Capital-backed, where the edge is

No loan spans two chains, so it needs inventory resting on each side, and its legs settle separately. It does not need to be first: the gaps it hunts last seconds to minutes, so it competes on correct pricing on both chains, control of the risk that one leg settles alone, and cheap inventory movement.

Plans live candidatesTwo-key leg reached the send on testnet
The solver layer · Inside engine 2

Moves the system's inventory, and later fills other people's orders

The cross-chain engine moves its inventory through the solver and bridge networks in their own order formats, reproduced exactly against their official tools. With enough inventory, the same capital can fill other people's cross-chain orders for a fee, so moving the system's own money earns instead of costing. Filling for others needs capital to front each fill, so it is built and switched off.

#04

Safety by construction

Enforced in code, not in review

Money only goes through doors

Value moves only through an exchange's or protocol's own entry functions, never by sending it to a contract address. This is checked when every trade is built.

No trade until every stage is green

A staged readiness check runs at start-up, from configuration and keys to custody and pricing. A skipped stage counts as red.

Stale prices are refused

Just before sending, the system asks the chain for its current block and refuses a trade priced too long ago. No setting loosens this past its cap. It is the check that stopped the first two-key trade, correctly.

Two keys for anything that can split

A trade whose parts can settle separately is co-signed by a second, independent service with its own rules, which checks every amount itself rather than trusting a claim about it.

An absence is never a number

A price it cannot read, a cost it cannot convert or a chain it cannot reach is recorded as exactly that, never as a zero or a default. Every instrument has to prove it could have found something before its silence counts.

Identity before trading

An asset is treated as the same on two chains only when the chains themselves prove it. A token that merely shares a name with a known one is refused.

#07

What is not there yet

Machine
No node servers

The node mode is built and has run on a development node. In development the system reads the chain over the network and falls behind fast chains.

Capital
No working capital on two chains

The cross-chain engine plans and checks real candidates, and stops where inventory is needed.

Builders
No block-builder route on Ethereum yet

Sending to Ethereum's block builders is the next integration. The contract already has the instruction that pays a builder's bid.

Later
Market making

Quoting both sides with owned inventory is the last phase, after the cross-chain record exists.
