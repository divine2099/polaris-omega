Architecture

One system, two engines, six layers.

Designed and built by Jamike Divine, working with an AI system: one Rust workspace of about thirty components, and one small contract on chain.

Built from the bottom up. A layer does not start until the one beneath it passes its tests.

Every part below is marked with what it actually is today: live, built but switched off, or designed.

Live Runs in the program and has run on the test network Built Written and tested, not switched on Designed Planned, not built
#01

The path of one trade

Top to bottom
InputA new block on any chain

On Ethereum, Base and Optimism the system runs as an extension inside the node software; on Arbitrum and BNB Chain it runs beside the node on the same machine and reads its live feed. Either way it sees new state as the node processes it.

↓
ReadPrices straight from the exchanges' contracts

No price feed in the way. Each chain keeps a running price estimate for every asset it watches.

↓
Engine 1A gap inside one chain

Two or more pools on the same chain disagree. One transaction can take it.

Engine 2A gap between chains

The same asset, proven to be the same asset, is priced differently on two chains.

↓
CheckSimulated exactly against the real state

The trade runs in a copy of the chain's own virtual machine. A cross-chain trade is simulated as each of its transactions.

↓
GateRisk limits, staleness and the right signer

A halt switch, a gas ceiling, a capital limit and the risk of one leg settling alone. A single-transaction trade signs with one key; anything that settles in parts needs a second, independent signer.

↓
ExecuteOne stateless contract

Borrows, swaps and repays inside one transaction. If it does not end with more than it started with, it undoes itself.

ExecuteA vault with a scoped role

Inventory sits in a Safe on each chain. The trading key may call a narrow set of functions and nothing else.

↓
RecordReceipt read back, result written down

Every attempt gets one record at the moment of sending and another when it resolves, so failures are counted too.

#02

Two engines

The edge is in the second
Engine 1 · Single chain

Flash-funded, profit or nothing

Takes gaps between pools on one chain, plus liquidations on lending markets, inside one transaction funded by a loan repaid in that same transaction. It needs no capital of its own, and on one chain it is a race to be first into the block. It is the entry point and the steady base.

Trading on testnet
Engine 2 · Between chains

Capital-backed, where the edge is

No loan spans two chains, so it needs inventory resting on each side, and its legs settle separately. It does not need to be first: the gaps it hunts last seconds to minutes, so it competes on correct pricing on both chains, control of the risk that one leg settles alone, and cheap inventory movement.

Plans live candidatesTwo-key leg reached the send on testnet
The solver layer · Inside engine 2

Moves the system's inventory, and later fills other people's orders

The cross-chain engine moves its inventory through the solver and bridge networks in their own order formats, reproduced exactly against their official tools. With enough inventory, the same capital can fill other people's cross-chain orders for a fee, so moving the system's own money earns instead of costing. Filling for others needs capital to front each fill, so it is built and switched off.

Order formats exactFills for others: off

#04

Knowing the market

What it trusts, and how it stays current

A pinned universe, built from the chains

Every pool and asset it may trade is read from the chains themselves at a fixed block and committed. A test rebuilds that universe from the chain and fails if the committed copy ever disagrees. Nothing outside it is traded.

Live

A watcher on every protocol it touches

On a fixed cadence it checks each pinned contract's code, its upgrade pointers and its key parameters. Any change stops trading on that venue until the new code is reviewed. Automatic re-review of the new code is the next step.

Watch and stop: liveAutomatic re-review: designed

Protocols read at source

Protocols are audited from their deployed source code, pinned to the exact version on chain and checked byte for byte against it, with every claim cited to a line of code. Most integrated venues are done, and a registry tracks the ones still queued. The watcher says when one needs reading again.

Most venues doneSome queued

Exact state at the moment of decision

Every trade is decided against the chain's actual state at that block, by running the real contract code rather than a model of the market. A decision priced too long ago is refused before it is sent.

Live

A running price estimate on every chain

Each chain keeps a filtered estimate of every asset's price, adjusted for decimals, direction and block time, so that two chains can be compared at all. Updating it on every block costs chain reads, so today it runs at start-up, and on every block once there is a node.

At start-upEvery block: built, off

A simulated multi-chain world

A test world built from real chain state on several chains at once, where a trade moves each pool exactly by that venue's own maths and rolls back for free. Its scoring refuses a strategy that only looks good on a lucky run. Rival bots, volatility and failure scenarios come next.

World and scoring: builtRivals and stress scenarios: designed

A copy of the chain is the ground truth for one block. The simulated world is built on those copies and adds what a copy cannot show: other players, stress and failure. Live decisions use neither; they use the chain itself at that block.

#05

Reaching the chain

Reading fast, sending privately
Read · Today

A paid endpoint at a hobby tier

Without a node, every read goes over the network to a hosted endpoint, paced against a monthly allowance and cached so nothing is paid for twice. It is enough to test on, and it is why the system falls behind a fast chain.

Live
Read · Target

Its own node on every chain

Inside the node on Ethereum, Base and Optimism; beside it on the same machine on Arbitrum and BNB Chain. State comes from the node's own database in well under a microsecond, with no network in the way.

Built, waits on a machine
Read · Bridge

Private endpoints until the nodes exist

A dedicated private endpoint per chain, with a real allowance, closes most of the gap before the nodes are up and stays as the fallback afterwards. Endpoint keys are never written into code or into any record.

The ask
Send · L2s

Straight to the sequencer

Base, Arbitrum and Optimism are ordered by a sequencer, not an auction. The system sends there directly, asks which route each endpoint actually accepts rather than assuming, and follows every transaction to its result.

Live on testnet
Send · Ethereum and BNB Chain

Private bundles through block builders

On chains with a block-builder auction, trades go as private bundles that land whole or not at all, and never sit in the public waiting room where others can copy or front-run them. Open builder networks such as BuilderNet come first, because any searcher can submit to them without a private deal and they refund part of the value a bundle adds. The contract already has the instruction that pays a builder's bid exactly; routing to builders is the next integration.

#06

The node servers

Where it will run

The system is meant to run on the node, not next to a farm of servers asking the node questions. Each chain gets its own bare-metal server, and the cores and memory beyond what the node needs are the system's own compute.

Ethereum

An execution node and a consensus node

The system runs as an extension inside the execution node, and private bundles to block builders will go out from here. It takes the other larger server.

Node mode: builtServer: the ask
Base · Optimism

One node each, the system inside it

Both run the same node software, which has an extension slot. Base gets one of the two larger servers, for the disk its chain needs.

Node mode: builtServer: the ask
Arbitrum · BNB Chain

The node, and the system beside it

These nodes have no extension slot, so the system runs as a second process on the same server, reading the node's live feed and producing the same signals as everywhere else.

Same signals: builtServer: the ask
Services

A separate machine for signing and coordination

The second signer, coordination between chains, the dashboard and the metrics run on their own small machine with no public address, apart from the trading servers.

BuiltMachine: the ask
Between them

A private network, snapshots and room to grow

The servers talk over a private, encrypted network; only the node's own peer-to-peer ports face the internet. Node snapshots are kept in object storage so a failed node is restored in hours rather than re-synced over days. Later, servers move next to each chain's sequencer, and the critical chains get a standby node.

Designed

About $1,600 a month runs four chains and the services machine; BNB Chain's node is heavier and adds a fifth server. Paying a year ahead brings the four-chain figure down by more than a quarter.

#07

Safety by construction

Enforced in code, not in review

Money only goes through doors

Value moves only through an exchange's or protocol's own entry functions, never by sending it to a contract address. This is checked when every trade is built.

No trade until every stage is green

A staged readiness check runs at start-up, from configuration and keys to custody and pricing. A skipped stage counts as red.

Stale prices are refused

Just before sending, the system asks the chain for its current block and refuses a trade priced too long ago. No setting loosens this past its cap. It is the check that stopped the first two-key trade, correctly.

Two keys for anything that can split

A trade whose parts can settle separately is co-signed by a second, independent service with its own rules, which checks every amount itself rather than trusting a claim about it.

An absence is never a number

A price it cannot read, a cost it cannot convert or a chain it cannot reach is recorded as exactly that, never as a zero or a default. Every instrument has to prove it could have found something before its silence counts.

Identity before trading

An asset is treated as the same on two chains only when the chains themselves prove it. A token that merely shares a name with a known one is refused.

#10

What is not there yet

Machine
No node servers

The node mode is built and has run on a development node. In development the system reads the chain over the network and falls behind fast chains.

Capital
No working capital on two chains

The cross-chain engine plans and checks real candidates, and stops where inventory is needed.

Builders
No block-builder route on Ethereum yet

Sending to Ethereum's block builders is the next integration. The contract already has the instruction that pays a builder's bid.

Later
Market making

Quoting both sides with owned inventory is the last phase, after the cross-chain record exists.
