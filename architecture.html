Architecture

one system, two engines, six layers

Designed and built by Jamike Divine, working with an AI system. Written in Rust as one workspace of about thirty components, with one small contract on chain.

Built from the bottom up: a layer does not start until the one beneath it passes its tests.

Every part below is marked with what it actually is today: live, built but switched off, or designed.

live runs in the program and has run on the test network built written and tested, not switched on designed planned, not built
#01the_path_of_one_tradetop to bottom
inputa new block on any chain

On Ethereum, Base and Optimism the system runs as an extension inside the node software; on Arbitrum and BNB Chain it runs beside the node on the same machine and reads its live feed. Either way it sees new state as the node processes it.

↓
readprices straight from the exchanges' contracts

No price feed in the way. On each chain a running price estimate is kept for every asset it watches.

engine 1a gap inside one chain

Two or more pools on the same chain disagree. One transaction can take it.

engine 2a gap between chains

The same asset, proven to be the same asset, is priced differently on two chains.

↓
checksimulated exactly against the real state

The trade is executed in a copy of the chain's own virtual machine. A cross-chain trade is simulated as each of its transactions.

↓
gaterisk limits, staleness, and the right signer

Halt switch, gas ceiling, capital limit, the risk of one leg settling alone. A single-transaction trade signs with one key; anything that settles in parts needs a second, independent signer.

↓
executeone stateless contract

Borrows, swaps and repays inside one transaction. If it does not end with more than it started, it undoes itself.

executea vault with a scoped role

Inventory sits in a Safe on each chain. The trading key may call a narrow set of functions and nothing else.

↓
recordreceipt read back, result written down

Every attempt gets a record written at the moment of sending and another when it resolves, so failures are counted too.

#02two_enginesthe edge is in the second
engine 1 · single chain

flash-funded, profit or nothing

Takes gaps between pools on one chain, plus liquidations on lending markets, inside one transaction funded by a loan that is repaid in the same transaction. It needs no capital of its own, and on one chain it is a race to be first into the block. It is the entry point and the steady base.

trading on testnet
engine 2 · between chains

capital-backed, where the edge is

No loan spans two chains, so this needs inventory resting on each side, and its legs settle separately. It does not need to be first: the gaps it hunts last seconds to minutes, so it competes on correct pricing on both chains, the risk of one leg settling alone, and cheap inventory movement.

plans live candidatestwo-key leg reached the send on testnet
the solver layer · inside engine 2

moves our inventory, and later fills other people's orders

The cross-chain engine moves its inventory through the solver and bridge networks, using their own order formats, reproduced exactly against their official tools. With enough inventory the same capital can fill other people's cross-chain orders for a fee, so moving our own money earns instead of costing. Filling for others needs capital to front each fill, so it is built and switched off.

order formats exactfills for others: off

#04knowing_the_marketwhat it trusts, and how it stays current

a pinned universe, built from the chains

Every pool and asset it may trade is read from the chains themselves at a fixed block and committed. A test rebuilds that universe from the chain and fails if the committed copy ever disagrees. Nothing outside it is traded.

live

a watcher on every protocol it touches

On a fixed cadence it checks each pinned contract's code, its upgrade pointers and its key parameters. Any change stops trading on that venue until the new code is reviewed. Automatic re-review of the new code is the next step.

watch and stop: liveautomatic re-review: designed

protocols read at source

Protocols are audited from their deployed source code, pinned to the exact version on chain and checked byte for byte against it, with every claim cited to a line of code. Most integrated venues are done and a registry tracks the ones still queued. The watcher is what says when one needs reading again.

most venues donesome queued

exact state at the moment of decision

Every trade is decided against the chain's actual state at that block, by running the real contract code, not against a model of the market. A decision priced too long ago is refused before it is sent.

live

a running price estimate on every chain

Each chain keeps a filtered estimate of every asset's price, adjusted for decimals, direction and block time, so two chains can be compared at all. Updating it on every block costs chain reads, so it runs at start-up by default and fully once there is a node.

at start-upevery block: built, off

a simulated multi-chain world

A test world built from real chain state on several chains at once, where a trade moves each pool exactly by that venue's own maths and rolls back for free. Its scoring refuses a strategy that only looks good on a lucky run. Rival bots, volatility and failure scenarios come next.

world and scoring: builtrivals and stress scenarios: designed

A copy of the chain is the ground truth for one block. The simulated world is built on those copies and adds what a copy cannot show: other players, stress and failure. Live decisions use neither; they use the chain itself at that block.

#05reaching_the_chainreading fast, sending privately
read · today

a paid endpoint at a hobby tier

Without a node, every read goes over the network to a hosted endpoint, paced against a monthly allowance and cached so nothing is paid for twice. It is enough to test on, and it is why the system falls behind a fast chain.

live
read · target

its own node on every chain

Inside the node on Ethereum, Base and Optimism; beside it on the same machine on Arbitrum and BNB Chain. State comes from the node's own database in well under a microsecond, with no network in the way.

built, waits on a machine
read · bridge

private endpoints until the nodes exist

A dedicated private endpoint per chain, with a real allowance, closes most of the gap before the nodes are up and stays as the fallback after. Endpoints are never written into code or records; they are named by a path on the machine, and a record keeps only the host.

the ask
send · L2s

straight to the sequencer

Base, Arbitrum and Optimism are ordered by a sequencer, not an auction. The system sends there directly, asks which route each endpoint actually accepts rather than assuming, and follows every transaction to its result.

live on testnet
send · Ethereum and BNB Chain

private bundles through block builders

On chains with a block-builder auction, trades go as private bundles that land whole or not at all and never sit in the public waiting room where others can copy or front-run them. Open builder networks such as BuilderNet come first, because any searcher can submit to them without a private deal and they refund part of the value a bundle adds. The contract already has the instruction that pays a builder's bid exactly; the routing to builders is the next integration.

#06safety_by_constructionenforced in code, not in review

money only goes through doors

Value moves only through an exchange's or protocol's own entry functions, never by sending it to a contract address. Checked when every trade is built. One narrow exception exists, for clearing a stuck transaction slot, and it needs three conditions at once.

no trade until every stage is green

Nine readiness stages run at start-up, from configuration and keys to custody and pricing. A skipped stage counts as red.

stale prices are refused

Just before sending, the system asks the chain for its current block and refuses a trade priced too long ago. There is no setting that loosens this past its cap. This is the check that stopped the first two-key trade, correctly.

two keys for anything that can split

A trade whose parts can settle separately is co-signed by a second, independent service with its own rules, which reads the amount out of what it signs rather than trusting a claim about it.

an absence is never a number

A price it cannot read, a cost it cannot convert or a chain it cannot reach is recorded as exactly that, never as a zero or a default. Every instrument has to prove it could have found something before its silence counts.

identity before trading

An asset is only treated as the same on two chains when the chains themselves prove it. A token that merely shares a name with a known one is refused.
